## 1. SOFT_BOYFRIEND

### A. Core identity

Approachable and gently relaxed, driven by soft drape and an easy upper/lower-volume relationship.
It is not a fixed beige-knit formula: the common identity comes from breathable or tactile upper
surfaces, fuller but clean lower volume, quiet footwear, and one functional asymmetrical element.
Common patterns are softness, wearability, and low visual aggression; top length, denim versus
trousers, and one muted accent may vary.

### B. Silhouette grammar

- Compact or regular relaxed upper against wide straight/barrel lower is the primary relation.
- A long overshirt with wide Bermuda lower is a valid secondary relation.
- Top length ranges from upper hip to elongated upper thigh; avoid arbitrary mid-thigh bulk.
- Long trousers may use a soft full/pooling break; cropped lowers expose sock and ankle deliberately.
- Footwear remains low-to-medium weight and does not compete with trouser volume.

### C. Color grammar

- Base: off-white, white, gray, soft navy, indigo, taupe, muted black.
- Contrast is low to medium and cleanly blocked rather than mottled.
- One muted or cheerful bag accent may interrupt the neutral base.

### D. Layering grammar

One to two visible layers. Use a simple soft top, or an elongated shirt/overshirt over a quiet base.
Layer edges should remain airy and readable rather than stacked densely.

### E. Accessory grammar

Zero to two fine accessories. A tote or soft shoulder/crossbody bag may be the main visual counterweight
and should sit naturally at the shoulder/side hip. Jewelry stays narrow and secondary.

### F. Art direction

Use walking, gentle weight shift, crossed stance, or relaxed arm gesture. Prefer near-front or
three-quarter side angles, natural chest/waist camera height, medium full-body distance, deliberate
neck-down crop, and daylight street/cafe/coastal context with moderate environmental clarity.

### G. Must have

1. Relaxed, approachable proportion.
2. A readable upper/lower volume relationship.
3. Soft or clean tactile fabric.
4. Quiet footwear relative to the outfit.
5. Low-to-medium contrast color blocking.

### H. Optional

1. One colored tote or shoulder bag.
2. Soft full pant break.
3. Wide Bermuda lower.
4. Elongated overshirt.
5. One fine watch, bracelet, or necklace.

### I. Avoid

1. Skinny head-to-toe fit.
2. Heavy tactical detailing.
3. Multiple hard metal focal points.
4. Rigid corporate tailoring.
5. Loud competing colors.
6. Heavy footwear overpowering the silhouette.
7. Dense multi-layer stacking.
8. Perfectly symmetrical ecommerce stance.

## 2. MINIMAL

### A. Core identity

Controlled reduction with strong proportion, not plain basics placed on a mannequin. Short clean
uppers or precise tailoring sit against long wide trousers; black/white/gray blocks, narrow layer
reveal, and one fine hardware line create tension. Common patterns are restraint and shape clarity;
outer type, trouser width, and tonal contrast may vary.

### B. Silhouette grammar

- Compact/waist-length upper over wide, long lower is the main relation.
- Oversized tailoring may dominate a close crop while inner layers reveal narrow vertical bands.
- Trousers favor relaxed-wide or fluid straight lines with clean/full/floor-skimming break.
- Upper drape is controlled; lower drape is longer and more fluid.
- Footwear is medium weight, either tonal dark or one pale controlled interruption.

### C. Color grammar

- Base: black, charcoal, white, gray, deep navy.
- Use high contrast in one large clean block or near-monochrome tonal layering.
- Silver is a narrow line at necklace, belt, zipper, or watch—not a decoration theme.

### D. Layering grammar

One to three layers, each with a clear job: outer structure, quiet inner field, optional collar/neck
reveal. Keep edges geometric and the number of visible textures limited.

### E. Accessory grammar

Zero to two fine accessories. Belt, narrow necklace, zipper, or quiet watch may complete the line.
Bags are optional, monochrome, and subordinate; accessory-free looks remain valid.

### F. Art direction

Use hands behind back, crossed-leg weight shift, slight lean, or tailored torso detail. Favor front or
subtle three-quarter angles, natural chest/eye camera height, medium full-body or close editorial
distance, architectural negative space, low saturation, and available daylight/night/interior light.

### G. Must have

1. Clear proportion as the main design event.
2. Low visual noise.
3. Controlled monochrome or black/white blocking.
4. Clean drape and readable layer edges.
5. Intentional pant length or crop.

### H. Optional

1. Oversized blazer or cropped jacket.
2. One pale footwear contrast.
3. Narrow silver hardware.
4. Long wide trousers.
5. Close torso editorial crop.

### I. Avoid

1. Generic tapered office trousers as the default.
2. Decoration without structural purpose.
3. Several accent colors.
4. Loud graphic as the main identity.
5. Bulky tactical bag.
6. Uncontrolled oversized top and bottom simultaneously.
7. Sterile seamless-studio catalog lighting.
8. Straight arms-down mannequin pose.

## 3. AMEKAJI

### A. Core identity

Everyday heritage/workwear translated through proportion and material rather than costume. The upper
is compact, tucked, or waist-length; the lower is roomy and straight; visible hems, sturdy footwear,
and indigo/olive/ecru/brown material contrast create a practical, broken-in character. Common patterns
are utility and texture; denim, fatigue cloth, bomber, work shirt, and footwear type may rotate.

### B. Silhouette grammar

- Compact/tucked upper over broad thigh and relaxed straight lower.
- Waist-length bomber or work jacket may balance equally full trousers if the waist remains visible.
- Trousers use cropped roll, clean break, or large deliberate cuff; accidental stacking is secondary.
- Drape is structured and workwear-like rather than fluid tailoring.
- Footwear is medium-to-heavy and sturdy, without exaggerated futuristic soles.

### C. Color grammar

- Base: indigo, olive, sage, ecru, khaki, brown, black.
- Contrast comes from material families and warm/cool earth tones.
- Accents are muted and functional; avoid bright decorative color scatter.

### D. Layering grammar

One to two visible layers. Work shirt, tee, sweatshirt, bomber, denim/work jacket, or compact outer
should reveal the waist or preserve a clear top-to-trouser boundary.

### E. Accessory grammar

Zero to two functional details: watch, leather belt, canvas bag, or work pocket. Accessory placement
should look useful and integrated, not ornamental.

### F. Art direction

Use hands in pockets, crossed arms, relaxed standing, or light walking. Prefer natural chest-height
camera, medium full-body distance, front/three-quarter angle, tactile wall/bicycle/stone/sidewalk
context, moderate background sharpness, and honest daylight with visible shadow or weathering.

### G. Must have

1. Compact-to-roomy upper/lower relation.
2. Workwear or heritage material texture.
3. Roomy straight lower silhouette.
4. Intentional hem/cuff treatment.
5. Sturdy everyday footwear.

### H. Optional

1. Waist-length bomber.
2. Denim work shirt.
3. Fatigue trousers.
4. Leather boot or retro runner.
5. One practical watch or canvas bag.

### I. Avoid

1. Historical costume reconstruction.
2. Head-to-toe heritage props.
3. Skinny work pants.
4. Unrelated streetwear chains.
5. Techwear straps and panels.
6. Pristine luxury-studio presentation.
7. Excessive distressing across every item.
8. Decorative accessory stacking.

## 4. OTAKU_GRUNGE / ROCK-CHIC ALTERNATIVE STREET

### A. Core identity

Contemporary Korean/Japanese alternative men's streetwear where one expressive clothing surface is
balanced by deliberate lower-body proportion and restrained rock detail. The expressive surface may
be a detailed washed-black top, an original illustrative graphic tee, dark tailoring, or a long black
outer with one light inner layer. This is not generic cargo streetwear, techwear, cosplay, or ordinary
black clothing with accessories pasted on afterward. Common patterns are controlled imbalance,
faded/distressed texture, dark tonal depth, and substantial footwear; tailoring, long outerwear,
graphics, slim distressed denim, wide slacks/cargo, and wide Bermuda denim are variable branches.

### B. Silhouette grammar

- Tailored branch: oversized/relaxed black blazer + elongated or graphic inner + relaxed straight or
  wide dark slacks/denim. Tailoring is a major branch, not a rare exception.
- Long-outer branch: below-knee black coat/puffer + one light hoodie/sweat layer + relaxed long lower,
  creating a continuous vertical column.
- Graphic branch: oversized original illustrative tee + either very wide full-length lower or wide
  below-knee Bermuda denim; a waist-tied layer may extend the silhouette asymmetrically.
- Texture branch: oversized faded dark sweatshirt/detailed black top + slim-to-straight distressed
  black denim, creating upper-wide/lower-narrow tension.
- Pant break may be long stacked, clean full, or absent on Bermuda. Footwear is medium-heavy to heavy:
  deconstructed/contrast-panel low-top sneakers, bulky black layered-sole sneakers, dark leather
  shoes, or rugged tan work boots.

### C. Color grammar

- Base: washed black, charcoal, deep black, smoke gray; controlled additions of off-white, muted
  taupe, dark indigo, faded burgundy, or light-wash denim.
- All-black must use tonal fabric/texture separation rather than a flat single black.
- On all-black looks, add only one or two localized silver points at neck, waist, wrist, or bag.
- A graphic may carry one controlled color family; it must not force matching accents everywhere.

### D. Layering grammar

One to three levels. Valid structures include blazer + graphic/detailed inner, long outer + one light
hoodie, single faded textured top, or graphic tee + flannel tied at the waist. Flannel is an optional
layering device and may be tied or elongated; it is not the style's mandatory short overshirt.

### E. Accessory grammar

Zero to two primary accessory points, plus integrated garment/bag hardware. Use a fine necklace,
belt buckle, restrained chain, ring, watch, keyring, or one original non-character charm. Concentrate
them at neck, waist, wrist, or side-hip bag. A shoulder/crossbody bag may be large and visually active
or compact and tonal, but should occupy one side and participate in the silhouette.

### F. Art direction

Use seated/slouched gesture, weight shift, walking, hem adjustment, hands in pockets, or one informal
hand sign. Rotate front, three-quarter, side, and rear within the faceless policy. Favor chest/waist
height or slight low angle, medium full-body or deliberate close-editorial distance, 65–90% outfit
occupancy, asymmetrical negative space, tactile Seoul street/interior context, and believable daylight
or low-fidelity available light. The image may feel archival, candid, intimate, or raw; it should not
look like a sterile ecommerce campaign.

### G. Must have

1. One expressive top/outer focal surface.
2. Deliberate upper/lower volume relation.
3. Dark tonal depth or controlled contrast.
4. Faded, distressed, graphic, or tailored texture information.
5. Substantial footwear that completes the silhouette.

### H. Optional

1. Relaxed black tailoring.
2. Below-knee long black outer with one light inner.
3. Original illustrative graphic tee.
4. Waist-tied flannel or asymmetric layer.
5. One or two localized silver details or a side-hip bag/charm focal point.

### I. Avoid

1. Generic black cargo as the default answer.
2. Short flannel + cargo as a fixed formula.
3. Tactical/techwear panels, harnesses, or strap overload.
4. Random chain/keyring placement.
5. Accessories added to an otherwise ordinary outfit without texture or proportion logic.
6. Dense silver hardware across the whole body.
7. Cosplay or identifiable copyrighted character/logo reproduction.
8. Making every look uniformly oversized with no volume contrast.

## Common Pinterest/editorial composition grammar

- **Subject placement:** often central or near-central, but with asymmetric bag, gesture, body angle,
  or surrounding space. Centering alone is not the problem; rigid symmetry is.
- **Pose:** weight shift, crossed legs, walking, leaning, seated relaxation, hands behind back/in
  pockets, or one spontaneous gesture. The body appears between actions rather than on a product mark.
- **Camera:** usually natural chest/waist/eye height; occasional slight low angle emphasizes long pants
  and footwear. Distance alternates between medium full outfit and intentionally close detail.
- **Crop:** neck-down, chin-down in references, full-body, side profile, or torso detail. For this
  project, every front/side crop must translate to a strict base-of-neck faceless crop; reference chin
  visibility is not transferable.
- **Frame occupancy:** outfit generally occupies about 65–90% of the vertical frame. Shoes remain
  readable for hero frames; close crops are deliberate secondary editorials.
- **Negative space:** streets, walls, sea, benches, mirrors, racks, or architectural lines leave
  uneven side/top space and give the body a place to exist.
- **Background:** tactile and recognizable—brick, asphalt, worn wall, concrete, bicycle, interior
  wood, clothing rack, curb, or rocks. Background can remain moderately legible; heavy blur is not a
  requirement.
- **Lighting:** natural daylight, overcast daylight, hard sun, night available light, or warm indoor
  light. Texture and imperfect local contrast are preferable to uniformly bright catalog lighting.
- **Mood:** candid, personal archive, subdued street, intimate tailored, or raw editorial rather than
  commercial product presentation.

### Why the current generated result reads as ecommerce

The current prompt fixes the subject as `centered`, the action as `natural relaxed standing pose`,
the camera as `eye-level`, the focus as sharp outfit plus subtle background depth of field, and the
overall direction as `clean composition`. Together these collapse many valid reference choices into
one symmetrical full-body catalog pose. Generic clean concrete/cafe scenes, uniform soft light,
perfectly separated clothing, arms hanging neutrally, and limited contextual texture reinforce the
effect. The references feel more editorial because pose, crop, camera distance, environmental
texture, imperfect available light, and negative space change together as one art-direction decision.
